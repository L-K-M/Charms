# Retrospective

I explored three different experiences: Impression made choosing a character
an act of typesetting; Orbit made related forms a controlled procession; The
Fold made the passage from catalogue to writing visible. I chose Impression
because its extravagance earns its space. A large arrow is pleasurable to
look at, while the small specimen helps you judge whether it is the arrow you
actually want in a line of text.

The strongest detail is not the paper colour. It is the relationship between
the raised block, the proof and the real key-release gate. Holding Return
applies pressure; releasing resumes writing. A quick tap does exactly the
same useful job, without waiting for the theatre. Hover cannot steal that
choice, and the proof never borrows private document text to look convincing.

Evaluating the native app in motion changed the implementation. The first
identity-based fade could leave blank paper during fast queries. I replaced
it with an always-present specimen and a short stamping response. A character
picker should reveal the new choice immediately, not make you wait for an
animation to explain itself.

I stopped at the composing surface. Redesigning Settings, adding sound,
inventing a font browser or animating a flight into the document would have
weakened the idea or implied knowledge the app does not have. The taller
panel is a real tradeoff. Larger-text reflow, a VoiceOver listening pass,
input methods and broader runtime compatibility still need work before this
prototype should be treated as release-ready.
